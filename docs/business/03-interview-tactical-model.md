# B03 — Tactical principles, phases and collective interactions

> Fictional coaching interview; not authentic Ferguson dialogue.

### Q1. Is 4-3-3 inherently better than 4-4-2?
**Imagined answer:** "A formation tells you where men start; it doesn't tell you what they do."
Describe the tactical game model in **four moments**: organized possession; organized defending; attacking transition; defensive transition. Include set pieces as a distinct process.

### Q2. Where are systems won and lost?
**Imagined answer:** "At the spaces around the ball, and the spaces we leave behind."
Map the field into lanes (left wide, left half-space, centre, right half-space, right wide), depth bands (build, progress, final third), and defensive zones. Assess:
- Progression: passing access, overloads, third-man options, vertical compactness.
- Final third: width, occupation of box, runners, cutback/passing lanes.
- Defensive block: pressure/cover/balance, compactness, weak-side coverage.
- Rest defence: defenders available behind the ball during attacks.
- Transitions: time to counter-press, counter-attack routes, protection of central areas.

### Q3. Why are two excellent full-backs sometimes incompatible?
**Imagined answer:** "If both charge forward, ask who protects the centre when the ball is lost."
A role may fit an individual while breaking the unit. Evaluate full-back, winger, nearest midfielder and centre-back **together**. Show interaction penalties as understandable reasons, not mysterious numbers.

### Q4. How does a high line relate to pressing?
Higher defensive line + weak pressure on the ball + slow recovery defenders = risk of passes into space. A product should flag this conditional risk; it cannot state goals will be conceded without match simulation and evidence. Separate trigger press, block height, line height and counter-press.

### Q5. How should tactics adapt between games?
Start with baseline game model. Then add opponent plan: protect opponent's strongest transition lane, choose matchups, set ball progression target, and adjust for fatigue/absence. Preserve a fallback plan and substitution rules.
