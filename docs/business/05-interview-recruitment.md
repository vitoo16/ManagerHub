# B05 — Recruitment, transfers and selling decisions

> Fictional manager interview, not real Ferguson testimony.

### Q1. What is the first question in scouting?
**Imagined answer:** "What football problem is this signing supposed to solve?"
Start from a named tactical or depth deficiency; write the target role before filtering the market.

### Q2. Is the biggest-name player the best signing?
Evaluate role-fit delta, improvement over available starters, versatility, age curve assumptions, availability, contract length, transfer fee, agent/sign-on fees, wages and opportunity cost. PA and reputation are insufficient.

### Q3. How should we price the decision?
Use scenarios of total cash exposure and annual cost. Distinguish annual wage, amortized fee for accounting-style planning, contractual cashflows and game-specific financial figures. Do not invent unknown instalments/clauses. Inputs with unknown coverage remain ranges or are excluded with a warning.

### Q4. When should an older star be sold?
**Imagined answer:** "The worst sale is the one made a season too late — but you must have a successor."
Check current contribution, successor readiness, wage freed, squad influence, schedule, market demand, replacement cost and team stability. Display both monetary and sporting impact.

### Q5. What makes shortlisting credible?
Each recommendation must show a **need statement**, minimum attributes, non-negotiables, confidence, evidence of price, and why cheaper alternatives were rejected. Support manual manager preferences.

**Example acceptance test:** A 19-year-old wonderkid with superior PA is not automatically recommended over a 27-year-old ready starter for an urgent relegation fight. Time horizon changes the recommendation.

**Outputs:** gap-driven shortlist, target ranking, sale/keep/loan choices, squad financial projection, audit log.
