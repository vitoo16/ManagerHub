# B07 — Training, fitness, workload and injury availability

> Fictional coaching conversation, not medical advice or real interview.

### Q1. Who should train hardest?
**Imagined answer:** "The player who can absorb it, for the purpose that improves the team."
Separate team tactical preparation, positional training, individual technical work, recovery and return-to-play. Intensity is constrained by match density, fatigue, injuries and medical staff assessments.

### Q2. How should minutes be distributed?
Analyze last N matches, minutes, travel/fixture congestion, injury status and rotation cost. High minutes **can indicate** load; without robust data, do not claim an exact injury probability.

### Q3. Can the app predict injuries?
Only as a **risk indicator** if validated on sufficiently representative data; otherwise label as qualitative load flags: multiple games in short window, recent injury, low condition. Never provide diagnosis.

### Q4. How should a player return?
Respect in-game medical eligibility and staff advice, introduce minutes progressively, consider role demands. The app must not override an unavailable/suspended flag purely to maximize XI score.

### Q5. What is a successful training block?
Training objectives should connect to tactical weakness and measurable change, but attribute gains take time. Track baseline snapshot, intervention, time elapsed, minutes and confounders; avoid false cause/effect.

**Outputs:** readiness board, rotation schedule, availability flags, training priorities and change timeline.
