# B08 — Matchday preparation, opposition analysis and in-match decisions

> Fictional management interview only.

### Q1. What belongs in a match plan?
**Imagined answer:** "Know how you will win, what could hurt you, and what you do when the plan fails."
Opponent shape (if observed), build-up patterns, defensive weak areas, dangerous transitions, set-piece threats, our XI, alternates and contingency substitutes.

### Q2. Can pre-match XI optimization ignore opponents?
No. Role/matchup utility is context-specific: fast wide opponent, aerial target, deep block, congested midfield, weather where provided. If opposition data unavailable, recommend based on baseline with confidence qualifier.

### Q3. When should the manager change shape?
Use observations, not scoreboard alone. Analyze possession entries, box access, turnovers, field position, shot locations and fatigue when available. Do not interpret a 0–1 score as proof the tactical system is failing.

### Q4. How should substitutions be planned?
Three categories: injury/availability, preserving structure, deliberately altering match state. When replacing a midfielder, recompute interaction/coverage; a high rated substitute is not automatically best.

### Q5. How does post-match analysis improve the team?
Record prediction, game plan, actual evidence and what changed. Separate:
- **What happened** (events and metrics).
- **Why it might have happened** (hypotheses).
- **What to change** (testable next-match adjustment).

**Outputs:** opponent dossier, pre-match XI A/B/C, substitution triggers, post-match learning log. Requires match data with actual coverage; no invented xG if absent.
