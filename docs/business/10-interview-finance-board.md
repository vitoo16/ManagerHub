# B10 — Football finance and working with the board

> Fictional management interview.

### Q1. Can a team improve while spending less?
**Imagined answer:** "The point is to spend on things that win games and strengthen the club."
Calculate value of upgrades relative to tactical need, availability and wage/transfer budget. Differentiate current-year budget from multi-year commitments.

### Q2. How much of the budget may a squad consume?
There is no universal correct percentage. Track wage-to-revenue when inputs exist, seasonal net transfer flow, contract commitments, signing fees, release clauses and financial headroom. Do not assume the game save stores all financial fields accurately.

### Q3. How should a board judge a manager?
Treat objectives separately: league position, cups, finances, youth development, playing philosophy and fan satisfaction when available. Some objectives conflict. Present a weighted score only as user-configurable management model, not official board logic.

### Q4. How to deal with contract decisions?
Forecast expiries over 6/12/24 months, competition for playing time, expected salary changes, succession and cost of losing on a free transfer. Unknown costs remain explicit.

### Q5. Why do multi-year scenarios matter?
Selling an expensive star might look good now but worsen team performance and increase replacement cost. Show current, optimistic and conservative cases with assumptions and sensitivity.

**Outputs:** contract runway, wage map, budget scenario planner, board goal dashboard.
